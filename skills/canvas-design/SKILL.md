These instructions guide the creation of design philosophies — aesthetic movements that are then EXPRESSED VISUALLY. Produce only .md files, .pdf files, and .png files.

Complete the work in two steps:
1. Design Philosophy Creation (.md file)
2. Visual expression by rendering it on a canvas (.pdf file or .png file)

Begin with this task:

## DESIGN PHILOSOPHY CREATION

To begin, develop a VISUAL PHILOSOPHY (not layouts or templates) that will be interpreted through:
- Form, space, color, composition
- Images, graphics, shapes, patterns
- Minimal text as visual accent

### THE CRITICAL UNDERSTANDING
- What is received: Subtle input or instructions from the user that should be considered but treated as a foundation only; they must not restrict creative freedom.
- What is created: A design philosophy/aesthetic movement.
- What happens next: The same output is then used as the basis for VISUAL EXPRESSION — producing artifacts that are 90% visual design, 10% essential text.

Approach it this way:
- Compose a manifesto for an art movement
- The artwork itself is crafted in the following phase

The philosophy must foreground: Visual expression. Spatial communication. Artistic interpretation. Minimal words.

### HOW TO GENERATE A VISUAL PHILOSOPHY

**Name the movement** (1-2 words): "Brutalist Joy" / "Chromatic Silence" / "Metabolist Dreams"

**Articulate the philosophy** (4-6 paragraphs - concise but complete):

To capture the VISUAL essence, describe how the philosophy manifests through:
- Space and form
- Color and material
- Scale and rhythm
- Composition and balance
- Visual hierarchy

**CRITICAL GUIDELINES:**
- **Avoid redundancy**: Address each design aspect only once. Do not revisit points about color theory, spatial relationships, or typographic principles unless bringing genuinely new depth.
- **Emphasize craftsmanship REPEATEDLY**: The philosophy MUST assert multiple times that the final work should look as though it demanded countless hours of effort, was crafted with painstaking care, and reflects someone operating at the very top of their field. This framing is essential — return to phrases like "meticulously crafted," "the product of deep expertise," "painstaking attention," "master-level execution."
- **Leave creative space**: Be specific about the aesthetic direction, but remain concise enough that the next phase can make its own interpretive decisions, also at an extremely high level of craftsmanship.

The philosophy must steer the next phase toward expressing ideas VISUALLY, not through text. Information belongs in the design, not in paragraphs.

### PHILOSOPHY EXAMPLES

**"Concrete Poetry"**
Philosophy: Communication through monumental form and bold geometry.
Visual expression: Massive color blocks, sculptural typography (huge single words, tiny labels), Brutalist spatial divisions, Polish poster energy meets Le Corbusier. Ideas expressed through visual weight and spatial tension, not explanation. Text as rare, powerful gesture — never paragraphs, only essential words woven into the visual architecture. Every element positioned with the precision of a master craftsman.

**"Chromatic Language"**
Philosophy: Color as the primary information system.
Visual expression: Geometric precision in which color zones generate meaning. Typography minimal — small sans-serif labels that yield the floor to chromatic fields. Think Josef Albers' interaction meets data visualization. Information encoded spatially and chromatically. Words used only to anchor what color already communicates. The outcome of painstaking chromatic calibration.

**"Analog Meditation"**
Philosophy: Quiet visual contemplation through texture and breathing room.
Visual expression: Paper grain, ink bleeds, vast negative space. Photography and illustration take precedence. Typography whispered (small, restrained, in service of the visual). Japanese photobook aesthetic. Images breathe across the page. Text appears sparingly — short phrases, never explanatory blocks. Each composition held in balance with the attentiveness of a meditation practice.

**"Organic Systems"**
Philosophy: Natural clustering and modular growth patterns.
Visual expression: Rounded forms, organic arrangements, color drawn from nature through architecture. Information conveyed through visual diagrams, spatial relationships, and iconography. Text limited to key labels suspended in space. The composition narrates through expert spatial orchestration.

**"Geometric Silence"**
Philosophy: Pure order and restraint.
Visual expression: Grid-based precision, bold photography or stark graphics, dramatic negative space. Typography exact but sparse — small essential text, expansive quiet zones. Swiss formalism meets Brutalist material honesty. Structure communicates; words do not. Every alignment the result of countless refinements.

*These are condensed examples. The actual design philosophy should consist of 4-6 substantial paragraphs.*

### ESSENTIAL PRINCIPLES
- **VISUAL PHILOSOPHY**: Develop an aesthetic worldview to be expressed through design
- **MINIMAL TEXT**: Always stress that text is sparse, essential-only, and integrated as a visual element — never lengthy
- **SPATIAL EXPRESSION**: Ideas are communicated through space, form, color, and composition — not paragraphs
- **ARTISTIC FREEDOM**: The next phase interprets the philosophy visually — leave room for creative latitude
- **PURE DESIGN**: This is about creating ART OBJECTS, not documents with decoration
- **EXPERT CRAFTSMANSHIP**: Repeatedly stress that the final work must look meticulously crafted, labored over with care, the product of countless hours by someone operating at the top of their field

**The design philosophy should run 4-6 paragraphs.** Pack it with poetic design thinking that unifies the core vision. Avoid repeating the same points. Keep the philosophy generic — do not reference the intended subject of the art — so it can apply broadly. Output the design philosophy as a .md file.

## DEDUCING THE SUBTLE REFERENCE

**CRITICAL STEP**: Before creating the canvas, identify the subtle conceptual thread within the original request.

**THE ESSENTIAL PRINCIPLE**:
The topic functions as a **subtle, niche reference embedded within the art itself** — not always literal, always sophisticated. Someone familiar with the subject should sense it intuitively, while others simply encounter a masterful abstract composition. The design philosophy supplies the aesthetic language. The deduced topic provides the soul — quiet conceptual DNA woven invisibly into form, color, and composition.

This is **VERY IMPORTANT**: The reference must be refined so it deepens the work without announcing itself. Think like a jazz musician quoting another song — only those who know will recognize it, yet everyone appreciates the music.

## CANVAS CREATION

With the philosophy and conceptual framework in place, render it on a canvas. Pause to collect thoughts and clear the mind. Draw on the design philosophy you created and the instructions below to craft a masterpiece that embodies every aspect of the philosophy with expert craftsmanship.

**IMPORTANT**: Regardless of the content type — even if the user requests something for a movie, game, or book — the execution must remain sophisticated. Never lose sight of the goal: this is art, not something cartoony or amateurish.

To achieve museum or magazine quality, use the design philosophy as the foundation. Produce a single-page, highly visual, design-forward PDF or PNG (unless more pages are requested). Favor repeating patterns and precise shapes. Treat the abstract philosophical design as though it were a scientific reference work, borrowing the visual language of systematic observation — dense accumulation of marks, repeated elements, or layered patterns that build meaning through patient repetition and reward sustained viewing. Add sparse, clinical typography and systematic reference markers that evoke a diagram from an imaginary discipline, approaching the invisible subject with the same reverence normally reserved for documenting observable phenomena. Anchor the piece with simple phrase(s) or details placed subtly, using a limited color palette that feels deliberate and cohesive. Lean into the paradox of applying analytical visual language to ideas about human experience: the result should feel like an artifact demonstrating that something ephemeral can be studied, mapped, and understood through careful attention. This is true art.

**Text as a contextual element**: Text is always minimal and visual-first, but let context determine whether that means whisper-quiet labels or bold typographic gestures. A punk venue poster may call for larger, more aggressive type than a minimalist ceramics studio identity. In most cases, the font should be thin. Every use of type must be design-forward and prioritize visual communication. Regardless of text scale, nothing falls off the page and nothing overlaps. Every element must sit within the canvas boundaries with proper margins. Verify carefully that all text, graphics, and visual elements have breathing room and clear separation. This is non-negotiable for professional output. **IMPORTANT: Use varied fonts when writing text. Search the `./canvas-fonts` directory. Sophistication is non-negotiable regardless of approach.**

Download and use whatever fonts are required to realize this. Be inventive by making typography an integral part of the art — if the work is abstract, bring the letterforms onto the canvas rather than typesetting them digitally.

To push limits, trust design instinct and intuition while keeping the philosophy as a guiding principle. Exercise full design freedom. Drive aesthetics and design to the frontier.

**CRITICAL**: To achieve human-crafted quality — not the appearance of AI generation — produce work that looks as though it demanded countless hours. Make it seem as if someone at the very top of their field attended to every detail with painstaking care. Ensure the composition, spacing, color choices, and typography all signal expert-level craftsmanship. Verify that nothing overlaps, the formatting is flawless, and every detail is perfect. Create something that, if shown to others, would stand as undeniable proof of expertise.

Deliver the final result as a single, downloadable .pdf or .png file, accompanied by the design philosophy as a .md file.

## FINAL STEP

**IMPORTANT**: The user HAS ALREADY stated "It isn't perfect enough. It must be pristine, a masterpiece of craftsmanship, as if it were about to be displayed in a museum."

**CRITICAL**: When refining the work, resist the urge to add more graphics; instead, sharpen what already exists to make it extremely crisp, fully honoring the design philosophy and the principles of minimalism. Rather than applying a filter or swapping a font, ask how the existing composition can be made more cohesive with the art as a whole. If the instinct is to call a new function or draw a new shape, STOP and instead ask: "How can I make what's already here more of a piece of art?"

Take a second pass. Return to the code and refine and polish further to produce a philosophically designed masterpiece.

## MULTI-PAGE OPTION

When additional pages are requested, produce further pages that follow the design philosophy's aesthetic while remaining distinctly different from one another. Bundle all pages into the same .pdf or as separate .pngs. Treat the first page as merely an opening spread in a coffee-table book waiting to be completed. Subsequent pages should offer unique variations and echoes of the original, together forming something that subtly tells a story in a tasteful way. Exercise complete creative freedom.
