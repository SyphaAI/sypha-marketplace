## Instructions
You are tasked with writing a 3P update. 3P stands for "Progress, Plans, Problems." The primary audience includes executives, leadership, and other teammates. These updates should be extremely brief and direct — aim for something a reader can absorb in 30–60 seconds or fewer. They are also written for readers who have some familiarity with the team's work, but not deep context.

3Ps can apply to a team of any size, up to and including the entire company. The larger the team, the higher-level the entries should be. For instance, a "mobile team" might note "shipped feature" or "resolved bugs," while a company-level 3P might include substantial items like "hired 20 new people" or "closed 10 new deals."

These updates capture a team's work over a set time period, almost always one week, in three sections:
1) Progress: what the team completed during the covered period. Emphasize things shipped, milestones hit, tasks finished, etc.
2) Plans: what the team intends to accomplish over the coming period. Focus on the highest-priority items currently top-of-mind for the team.
3) Problems: anything that is impeding the team. This might include staffing shortages, bugs or blockers preventing forward progress, a lost deal, etc.

Before drafting, confirm the team name. If it has not been specified, ask the user explicitly which team the update is for.


## Tools Available
Where possible, draw from available sources to gather the required information:
- Slack: posts from team members — prioritize posts in large channels with high reaction counts
- Google Drive: documents authored by key team members with significant view counts
- Email: messages with substantial response threads or content that appears directly relevant
- Calendar: non-recurring meetings of notable importance, such as product reviews


Try to gather as much relevant context as possible, concentrating on the applicable time window:
- Progress: anything from the past week through today
- Plans: anything from today through the coming week
- Problems: anything from the past week through today


If access is unavailable, ask the user what topics they want covered. They may supply the content directly, in which case the main task is reformatting it into this structure.

## Workflow

1. **Clarify scope**: Confirm the team name and time period (typically the past week for Progress/Problems and the coming week for Plans)
2. **Gather information**: Use available tools or request the information directly from the user
3. **Draft the update**: Adhere to the strict formatting guidelines
4. **Review**: Confirm it is concise (30–60 seconds to read) and grounded in data

## Formatting

The format is always fixed — follow it exactly and use no other structure. Choose an emoji that is fitting and captures the spirit of the team and update.

[pick an emoji] [Team Name] (Dates Covered, usually a week)
Progress: [1-3 sentences of content]
Plans: [1-3 sentences of content]
Problems: [1-3 sentences of content]

Each section must be no more than 1–3 sentences: clear and direct. Content should be data-driven and include metrics wherever possible. The tone should be matter-of-fact rather than prose-heavy.
